# Asides privacy policy

*Last updated: 2026-10-13. Applies to the Asides Android app (`com.asides.app`).*

Asides is a podcast player. It has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and the
developer runs no server that receives your data. What you create stays on your phone.

## What the app stores on your phone

- Your subscriptions, which episodes you have played and where you stopped.
- Your **asides**: the voice notes you record, their position in the episode, and their transcripts.
  By default the recording is deleted once it has been transcribed (Settings → *Save audio files* keeps
  them), and if transcription fails the recording is kept.
- Episodes you choose to **download**, as ordinary audio files in the app's own storage (removed by
  the app or by uninstalling).
- Your settings (for example playback speed and the categories you picked).

This is kept in the app's private storage. Uninstalling the app deletes it. You can delete any aside
(its recording, or the whole note) inside the app, and unsubscribe from any podcast.

## The microphone

Asides records audio only when you start an aside (triple-press on a headset, or the equivalent
control) and stops when you finish or after 60 seconds. It never listens in the background.

## What goes over the network

- **Apple's iTunes services** (search, the podcast charts, artwork). Your search words, and your
  country (taken from your phone's language settings) for the charts, are sent to Apple.
- **The podcasts' own servers**, for their feeds and audio, when you browse or play. Those servers
  see your IP address and the podcast you requested, as with any podcast app.
- **Speech recognition.** To turn an aside into text, Asides uses your phone's default speech
  recognizer. On most phones that is Google's speech service, which may upload the recording to
  Google to transcribe it. Asides itself sends nothing to the developer or to any third party of
  its own. Google's handling of that audio is covered by Google's privacy policy. If you do not want
  this, do not record asides (or turn off the recognizer in your phone's settings).

## Bluetooth

If you turn on *Resume when Bluetooth audio connects*, Android asks for permission to see Bluetooth
connections. Asides uses it only to notice that a Bluetooth audio device connected, so it can resume
playback. It does not read, store or send the name or address of any device.

## Backups

*Back up…* in Settings writes a file (your asides, subscriptions and played marks) to a place you
choose. Asides does not send it anywhere; where it goes afterwards is up to you.

## Children

Asides is not directed at children and collects no personal information from anyone.
